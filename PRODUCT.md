# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo developers using `pi` locally on their own machines. They work across local workspaces, sessions, transcripts, diffs, terminals, provider/model settings, skills, and extensions. They need the app to help them stay oriented while delegating coding tasks to an agent, without turning the workflow into a heavyweight IDE.

## Product Purpose

`pi-gui` provides a Codex-style Electron desktop shell for local `pi` sessions. It exists to make local agent work easier to start, monitor, resume, and inspect through a focused desktop interface backed by the upstream `pi` runtime. Success means a developer can quickly choose a workspace, start or resume a thread, send prompts, understand what the agent did, inspect tool activity and diffs, and adjust settings with minimal friction.

## Brand Personality

Fast, technical, precise. The app should feel capable and workflow-oriented, with enough warmth to stay approachable for personal use. The tone should be direct, efficient, and confident rather than promotional or playful.

## Anti-references

Avoid sterile enterprise admin UI, flashy AI SaaS dashboard tropes, dense IDE-clone complexity, and toy-like creator-app styling. Do not let the warm visual style become unserious, and do not let the technical surface become cold or bureaucratic.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the agent workflow legible: make session state, active workspace, runtime activity, and next actions easy to understand at a glance.
2. Preserve local-tool confidence: the UI should feel dependable, fast, and precise enough for real development work.
3. Reduce IDE gravity: expose coding-agent context, diffs, terminals, and settings without recreating a full editor shell.
4. Favor useful warmth: rounded surfaces and friendly empty states can lower friction, but task clarity always wins over decoration.
5. Respect continuity: session history, cached transcripts, workspace context, and long-running work should feel persistent and safe.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No special accessibility constraints were specified. Default expectation is a keyboard-friendly, readable product UI with visible focus states, clear semantic color usage, and support for reduced-motion-safe interactions where motion is added.
